## Section A — Short Answer (3 marks)

**Q1. Define career planning and its significance in today's job market.**
Career planning is the ongoing process of setting career goals and mapping the steps — skills, education and experience — needed to reach them. It matters today because the job market changes fast: planning keeps you adaptable, focused and employable, helping you make informed choices instead of drifting.

**Q2. What is meant by transferable skills?**
Abilities that carry across jobs and industries — communication, teamwork, problem-solving, leadership, time management — not tied to one role, making career change and pivoting easier.

**Q3. Mention any three key components of the Flower Exercise.**
The Flower Exercise (from *What Color Is Your Parachute?*) has seven petals. Any three: transferable skills; favourite knowledge/interests; preferred people & environments; working conditions; level of responsibility & salary; preferred geography; purpose/values.

**Q4. List three reasons why people change careers.**
Any three: better pay/growth; job dissatisfaction or burnout; work–life balance or purpose/passion; redundancy or industry decline; relocation/life changes.

**Q5. Explain the concept of "career pivot."**
A strategic move into a new but related career that reuses your existing transferable skills and experience rather than starting over.

**Q6. What is the hidden job market?**
The large share of jobs filled without public advertising — through networking, referrals and direct approaches. Reaching it needs proactive contact, not just replying to ads.

**Q7. What are informational interviews?**
Informal conversations with people in a field/role to gather insight and advice (not to ask for a job), helping you explore careers and build your network.

**Q8. Name any two career self-assessment tools.**
Any two: Flower Exercise, MBTI, Strong Interest Inventory, StrengthsFinder, Holland Codes (RIASEC), skills inventory.

**Q9. What is meant by "building a bridge" in a career shift?**
Creating a transition path between your current and target careers — gaining skills, experience or contacts that connect the two so the move is gradual, not a sudden leap.

**Q10. What role do personal values play in career choice?**
Values (security, creativity, helping others, autonomy…) guide satisfying career decisions; aligning work with values raises motivation, engagement and long-term fulfilment.

## Section B — Paragraph/Problem (6 marks)

**Q11. Explain the Parachute Method and how it differs from traditional job hunting.**
A self-directed job hunt that starts with deep self-knowledge (Flower Exercise), then targets fields and organisations you choose and approaches them directly via networking and informational interviews. Unlike traditional job hunting (sending resumes to ads and waiting), it is proactive, person-centred and focused on fit.

**Q12. Discuss the importance of starting with self-knowledge before choosing a career.**
Knowing your skills, interests, values and personality first ensures a good job-fit, avoids later dissatisfaction, gives direction, boosts interview confidence and lets you market your strengths. Without it, choices become guesswork.

**Q13. Describe the process and benefits of conducting a Flower Exercise.**
Process: complete the seven petals (skills, interests, people/environments, working conditions, responsibility & salary, geography, purpose/values), then synthesise them into an ideal-work picture. Benefits: self-awareness, clear direction, better fit, stronger self-marketing, confident decisions.

**Q14. How can someone use the Two-Step Career Change strategy effectively?**
When the target differs in both field and role, change one at a time. Step 1: change industry OR job function (keep the other constant). Step 2: change the second from the new position. This lowers risk and makes a big transition achievable.

**Q15. Describe three steps to explore new career paths without quitting your current one.**
(i) Research + informational interviews; (ii) build skills via courses/side projects/volunteering; (iii) test with freelancing/part-time before committing — exploring safely while keeping income.

**Q16. Discuss how informational interviews help in career decision-making.**
They give insider information on a role's reality, skills and paths; reveal hidden openings and contacts; test fit; and build your network — leading to better-informed, confident decisions.

**Q17. What are common employer biases, and how can one handle them during job search?**
Biases: age, gender, appearance, employment-gap, over/under-qualification, culture-fit. Handle by focusing on skills/achievements/value, presenting confidently, addressing concerns proactively, getting referred, and targeting inclusive employers.

**Q18. Suggest methods to research companies when no job is posted.**
Company website and LinkedIn; news/reports/social media; informational interviews with current/former employees; industry events; and a direct, tailored approach to the organisation. Uncovers hidden openings and shows initiative.

## Section C — Essay (10 marks)

**Q19. "Career clarity is built, not found." Discuss using concepts and strategies.**
Intro: clarity is constructed through self-exploration and action, not found by waiting.
Body: (1) self-assessment (Flower Exercise, values, transferable skills) builds the base; (2) exploration via informational interviews/research tests ideas; (3) experimentation (courses, side projects, freelancing) turns assumptions into evidence; (4) reflection and iteration refine direction; (5) the Parachute Method shows clarity emerges from doing.
Example: someone unsure between fields does informational interviews and a side project, and clarity grows.
Conclusion: build clarity through self-knowledge and action rather than waiting to "find" it.

**Q20. Design a career shift plan (teaching → UX design) using Parachute Method, Flower Exercise and Two-Step Change.**
Intro: teaching→UX changes both field and role, so combine the three tools.
- Flower Exercise: map transferable skills (empathy, communication, explaining, organising, understanding learners as users).
- Parachute Method: choose target UX roles/companies and approach directly via informational interviews and networking.
- Two-Step Change: first pivot to edtech/instructional-design/content (bridge), then to UX design proper.
- Build the bridge: UX course, portfolio (app redesigns, case studies), volunteer/freelance UX work.
- Test & transition: UX side projects while teaching, then apply when portfolio and network are ready.
Conclusion: self-knowledge + proactive search + staged Two-Step move makes the shift realistic and low-risk.
