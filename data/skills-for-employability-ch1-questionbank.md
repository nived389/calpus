## Section A — Short Answer (2 marks)

**Q1. What is meant by a "disrupted" workplace?**
A workplace reshaped by rapid, continuous change — technology, automation, AI, and remote/hybrid work — so traditional roles, skills and job security keep evolving and workers must continually adapt.

**Q2. Why is self-compassion important during a job search?**
Job searching brings repeated rejection. Self-compassion (treating yourself with kindness instead of harsh self-criticism) protects motivation, mental health and confidence so you can keep applying and learning.

**Q3. Name two challenges of long-term unemployment.**
(i) Skill erosion and outdated experience; (ii) loss of confidence/motivation and employer stigma about the employment gap.

**Q4. What does "the elimination game" refer to in hiring?**
The early hiring stages where recruiters and ATS software screen OUT unsuitable applicants (by keywords, filters, criteria) rather than screen them in — so the first goal is to avoid being eliminated.

**Q5. Mention two strategies for overcoming job search procrastination. Define "long-term unemployment."**
Strategies: (i) break the search into small daily tasks/goals; (ii) set a fixed schedule with deadlines and accountability. Long-term unemployment = being jobless and actively seeking work continuously for 27 weeks (about 6 months) or more.

**Q6. Give one reason why employers prefer flexible work models.**
They widen the talent pool and cut costs (office space) while improving productivity, retention and employee wellbeing.

**Q7. What are employers looking for beyond technical skills?**
Soft/employability skills — communication, teamwork, adaptability, problem-solving, a positive attitude and resilience.

**Q8. Why is physical fitness important during job hunting?**
Job hunting is long and stressful; fitness sustains energy, focus, mood and confidence, helping you stay motivated and perform well in interviews.

**Q9. What is meant by "resilience" in a career context?**
The ability to adapt to setbacks, rejection and change — to recover quickly, stay motivated and keep moving toward your career goals.

## Section B — Paragraph (6 marks)

**Q10. Explain how employer expectations differ from job seeker assumptions.**
Job seekers often assume that a degree/qualification alone secures a job and that hiring is quick and purely merit-based. Employers instead expect proven soft skills, cultural fit, adaptability and clear evidence of value — not just credentials. They reward tailored applications, relevant experience and good communication. This mismatch causes frustration; closing it means researching the role, demonstrating skills with real examples, and aligning yourself to what the employer actually needs.

**Q11. Discuss the challenges of predicting a job hunt timeline.**
A timeline is hard to predict because it varies by industry, role and seniority; many jobs are hidden/unadvertised; hiring processes are long and multi-stage; economic conditions and hiring freezes intervene; competition and applicant numbers vary; and employer response times are unpredictable. Seekers should therefore plan for uncertainty, keep several applications active, and not lose heart over delays.

**Q12. How can candidates handle unconscious bias in interviews?**
Candidates can prepare strong, evidence-based answers focused on skills and results; build rapport with confident, positive body language; steer answers back to their competencies; research the company and interviewers; ask thoughtful questions; and follow up professionally. While removing bias is the employer's responsibility, presenting clear value and professionalism reduces its impact.

**Q13. Describe two ways to channel job search frustration positively.**
(i) Convert frustration into productive action — upskilling, networking, and improving the resume/portfolio. (ii) Use it for reflection and goal-setting, and release the emotion through exercise, hobbies or creative outlets to reset. This turns negative energy into momentum instead of burnout.

**Q14. How does goal setting improve job search outcomes?**
Clear, specific (SMART) goals give direction, break an overwhelming search into manageable steps, sustain motivation and let you track progress. Daily/weekly targets — applications sent, contacts made, skills learned — create accountability and a sense of achievement, improving consistency and results.

## Section C — Essay (10 marks)

**Q15. Analyze how the traditional career path has evolved in today's job market.**
Introduction: The old "linear" career — one employer, steady promotions, a job for life — has given way to dynamic, non-linear careers.
Body: First, technology and automation reshaped roles and demanded continuous reskilling. Second, the gig economy, freelancing and portfolio careers replaced single lifelong jobs. Third, remote and hybrid models broke geographic limits. Fourth, frequent job changes and lateral moves are now normal, and skills matter more than tenure. Fifth, employability now rests on adaptability and lifelong learning rather than a single qualification.
Example: a graduate who freelances, upskills online and moves across industries several times in a decade.
Conclusion: Success today depends on adaptability, continuous learning and resilience rather than climbing a fixed ladder.

**Q16. Describe ten ways to build resilience during a prolonged job hunt.**
Introduction: Resilience is the ability to keep going through rejection and delay. Ten ways to build it:
1. Set small, achievable daily goals.
2. Keep a routine and structure to your day.
3. Practise self-compassion instead of self-blame.
4. Stay physically fit and healthy.
5. Build a support network of family, friends and mentors.
6. Reframe rejection as feedback and a learning opportunity.
7. Keep upskilling to stay confident and current.
8. Celebrate small wins to maintain momentum.
9. Manage stress with mindfulness, breaks and hobbies.
10. Stay positive and focus only on what you can control.
Example: after many rejections, a candidate joins a course, exercises daily and tracks progress — and stays motivated until an offer comes.
Conclusion: Resilience turns a hard, prolonged job hunt into steady progress and eventual success.

## Practice Case Study — Ravi's Job Search Journey
Ravi is a recent business graduate applying for marketing assistant roles. He has had only a few interview calls and no offers. A mentor notes his resume is generic, his LinkedIn is not updated, he struggles to speak clearly in interviews, and he feels demotivated after rejections.
1. Identify three specific problems Ravi is facing in his job search. → generic resume; outdated/weak LinkedIn presence; poor interview communication (plus low motivation after rejection).
2. Suggest two strategies to improve his resume and online presence. → tailor the resume to each role with keywords and achievements; update LinkedIn with a clear headline, skills, projects and activity.
3. Techniques to boost interview confidence. → mock interviews and practice, prepare STAR answers, research the company, improve body language and delivery.
4. How can Ravi practise resilience against repeated rejection? → treat each rejection as feedback, keep a routine, set small goals, seek support, and keep upskilling.
5. Short mentor action plan (4–5 points). → (a) rewrite a targeted resume; (b) refresh LinkedIn; (c) do weekly mock interviews; (d) apply to a set number of tailored roles per week; (e) build a daily resilience/upskilling habit.
